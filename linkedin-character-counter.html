LinkedIn character limits explained

LinkedIn enforces character limits on every content field to keep profiles and posts readable. Posts are capped at 3,000 characters — enough for a detailed article-style update but short enough to maintain attention. The headline shown below your name is limited to 220 characters, which is your primary professional pitch visible across the platform. Your About section allows 2,600 characters for a longer personal narrative, while connection request notes are limited to just 300 characters.

Why hitting the limit matters

LinkedIn does not warn you until you actually hit the limit — it simply stops accepting input. If you draft your content outside LinkedIn (in a document or email) and paste it in, you may silently lose the end of your text. Using this counter before pasting lets you confirm you're within the limit and adjust your copy without any surprises.

Tips for staying within limits

For posts, the "see more" fold appears around 210 characters — put your strongest hook in the first two lines. Headlines should lead with your most searchable role or skill since LinkedIn's algorithm uses them for search ranking. In your About section, the first 300 characters are shown before the "see more" button, so treat them like a meta description for your profile.
